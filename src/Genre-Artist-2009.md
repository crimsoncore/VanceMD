# "The Genre Artist" — Carlo Rotella, New York Times Magazine, 2009
A summary and citation record for this profile, not a reproduction of it. The full text is copyrighted by The New York Times Company; only brief, clearly-attributed factual points are noted here. The complete article is held locally at `interview/The Genre Artist - NYTimes.com.pdf`.
## Citation
Carlo Rotella, "The Genre Artist," *New York Times Magazine*, 19 July 2009, p. MM20 of the New York print edition (pp. 20–25 as paginated in some citations). <https://www.nytimes.com/2009/07/19/magazine/19Vance-t.html>. Reprinted as the introduction to Humayoun Ibrahim's graphic-novel adaptation of *The Moon Moth* (First Second, 2012) and, in German, in *Jack Vance: Weltenschöpfer und Wortschmied*, ed. Andreas Irle (2016), 391–404.
Rotella interviewed Vance in person at his Oakland home in the winter of 2008–2009; the piece was occasioned by the publication of Vance's memoir *This Is Me, Jack Vance!* and the tribute anthology *Songs of the Dying Earth*, both released around the same time.
## What it covers
A profile-and-interview hybrid making the literary case for Vance as an underrated prose stylist, built around: appreciations from Michael Chabon, Dan Simmons, Neil Gaiman, and Tanith Lee (each describing their own adolescent discovery of Vance's prose); an account of the Vance Integral Edition and the Totality text-search tool; a description of Vance's late-life circumstances (blindness, living arrangements, working with his son and caretaker); biographical background (Depression-era childhood, Berkeley, pulp influences, marriage, travel, and writing habits); and reflections on why Vance's work resists easy critical placement between "genre" and "literary" categories.
