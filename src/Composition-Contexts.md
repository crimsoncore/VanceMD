# Composition Contexts: Where and When the Books Were Written

This page reconstructs, as far as the evidence allows, *where and roughly when* individual Vance titles were drafted — as opposed to when they were first published (for which see [Vol. Contents](./VIE.md)). The main source is Jack Vance's dictated autobiography, *This Is Me, Jack Vance!* (Gollancz/SF Gateway ebook, 2009; hereafter "the memoir"). Additional evidence comes from *Cosmopolis* #40–41 (July–August 2003), especially Norma Vance's biographical essays and Chuck King's report on the John Holbrook Vance Collection at Boston University's Mugar Library. Cross-references to publication dates are drawn from [`VIE.md`](./VIE.md), which is itself sourced from ISFDB and the FictionMags Index.

**This is a thin record, not a full bibliography-to-timeline mapping.** The memoir is a "landscape," in Vance's own words, not a writing-shop diary — it names the specific composition setting for only a handful of titles out of his ~90-item bibliography. The Cosmopolis material adds valuable context, especially about childhood landscape, house-building, and surviving manuscript evidence, but it usually does *not* identify a specific title being written at a specific place. Resist the temptation to fill those gaps by inference from travel dates alone — a period of travel is not evidence that a *specific* book was written during it unless a source says so.

## Confidence key

- 🟢 **Stated directly** — the memoir names the title and the place (and usually a rough time) together, explicitly.
- 🟡 **Bounded by context** — the place is explicit, but the *date* is inferred by chaining other explicit facts in the narrative (a preceding/following event's date, a stated age, a book's independently known publication date). Shown as `~year` or a range.
- 🔴 **External anchor only** — not from the memoir; included only to orient the chart (e.g., a publication date from VIE.md, used as a boundary condition).
- 🟣 **Manuscript-evidence context** — physical manuscript/draft evidence identifies relationships among drafts, working titles, or writing order, but not necessarily the place of composition.

## Contextual influences that are not composition claims

These are important for "what context was around the writing," but they are not evidence that any specific book was drafted at that place/time.

- **Oakley / Iron House School / Delta landscape** — Norma Vance says the move to the Oakley country ranch was, in Jack's mind, "the single most important event in his life"; she explicitly states that he remembered the slough-edge school, dairy, orchards, eucalyptus/firs/shrubs/flowers/weeds, dun soil, and Mount Diablo view, and that he "remembered it all when writing his books" as a resource for "descriptive prose: of place, of mood, and sensation." This is strong primary testimony for the childhood landscape as a lifelong descriptive reservoir, but not a claim about any one book. Source: Norma Vance, "A Jack Vance Biography," *Cosmopolis* #40 (July 2003), pp. 1–3.
- **Oakland house-building while writing** — Norma Vance describes the Valley View property as a rustic house on three hillside lots, with Jack creating a building site by pick, shovel, and wheelbarrow; "all the while Jack made time to write." She also describes him retreating to a self-dug fallout-shelter cave with a thermos, slingback chair, pillow/clipboard, fire and lantern to work when noise in the house made concentration difficult. This supports the general composition context "Oakland house under construction," especially for the 1960s–70s, but no title is named. Source: Norma Vance, "A Different View of Jack Vance," *Cosmopolis* #41 (Aug 2003), pp. 1–2.
- **Early manuscript around 1953** — *Cosmopolis* #41 reproduces a photo captioned "Norma with an early manuscript, circa 1953." Useful as visual/contextual evidence of the Vances' working partnership and manuscript-handling, but the title of the manuscript is not identified in the caption. Source: *Cosmopolis* #41 (Aug 2003), p. 5.
- **Houseboat date evidence** — Norma Vance's article captions a photograph "Houseboat with Albert Hall, Jack, Poul Anderson, circa 1962" and says the houseboat plan arose "when John was still a baby." This is stronger evidence for a c. 1962 construction date than later recollections that put the houseboat in the mid/late 1960s or c. 1972, but it does not directly identify any fiction written aboard the houseboat. Source: Norma Vance, "A Different View of Jack Vance," *Cosmopolis* #41 (Aug 2003), pp. 3–4.

## A likely (but unverified) identification worth flagging

The memoir credits "Seven Exits from Bocz" to "The Chowder and Marching Science Fiction Society of Berkeley," while `VIE.md` (via ISFDB/FictionMags) gives its first publication as *The Rhodomagnetic Digest*, v4 #2, 1952. These are probably the same venue under different names — *The Rhodomagnetic Digest* was the clubzine of a real Bay Area fan group historically known as "The Elves', Gnomes' and Little Men's Science Fiction Chowder and Marching Society," based in Berkeley — but that identification rests on background knowledge, not on a source re-checked in this session, so it should be verified before being promoted to `vance-research-log.md` as a settled fact.

> ***NOTE:*** Wil Ceron was so kind to check this, as he owns a copy of the 1952 *Rhodomagnetic Digest*, and confirmed that it does indeed contain "Seven Exits from Bocz" by the Chowder and Marching Science Fiction Society of Berkeley. This is now verified. It is The Rhodomagnetic Digest, Volume IV, issue #21, number 2, 1952. "The Elves', Gnomes' and Little Men's Science Fiction, Chowder and Marching Society is composed of people who are interested in reading, writing, or collecting science fiction and fantasy in any of its forms." - source: <https://fanac.org/fanzines/Rhodomagnetic/Rhodomagnetic21-00.html>

## What this page deliberately does *not* claim

- No composition context is asserted for the Demon Princes, Planet of Adventure (Tschai), Alastor Cluster, Durdane, Cadwal individual volumes, *Emphyrio*, *Big Planet*, *The Languages of Pao*, the Cugel/Rhialto Dying Earth sequels, the mysteries (Joe Bain, Ellery Queen house-name books), or the vast majority of the short fiction — the memoir simply does not say where or when these were written, beyond the general working rhythm described in the Final Word (longhand at home with colored fountain pens; a single pen plus a portable typewriter, with Norma typing successive drafts, while traveling).
- The three explicit travel-era "we settled in and worked" passages that name *no specific title* (the 1969 European trip, the 1974 round-the-world trip's three months on the houseboat "Bluebird" at Lake Nagin, Kashmir) are intentionally excluded from the table above, even though fan bibliographies sometimes associate that Kashmir stay with specific mid-1970s titles — the memoir itself does not make that link, so asserting it here would manufacture false precision.
